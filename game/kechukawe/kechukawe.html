How to play

  1. Players take turns rolling a five-sided die.
  2. Each roll fills that many empty spaces in your territory, up to 10.
  3. Once your territory is full, remaining points remove occupied spaces from the opponent's territory.
  4. After both territories have been filled at least once, the first player to clear the opponent's territory while keeping their own full wins.

Digital adaptation: The last rule and handling of excess points are simplified design choices for a complete playable game, not claims about a single historical ruleset.

About Kechukawe

Kechukawe is a traditional Mapuche game associated with a five-sided die called the kechukan. The Mapudungun word kechu means “five.” Accounts describe different ways of playing across times and places.

This prototype draws on the educational Aukantün description of filling one's own territory before removing the opponent's pieces. It is not a definitive reconstruction.
