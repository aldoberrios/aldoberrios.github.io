How to play

Default two-half game: Puelmapu (right) and Ngulumapu (left) each have 24 lofs. You control the entire right half; the AI controls the entire left half. You may place pieces anywhere in your half. In this mode the 24 lofs follow the straight dividing edge and outer curve of each semicircle with approximately even spacing. The four-quarter modes retain their original 12-lof arrangements, territory names and colours.

In four-territory modes, each territory has 12 positions: one at the pointed end, three along each straight side, and five around the outer curve and begins with one occupied lof. Roll 1–4 to place that many pieces in empty positions in your territory. Once it is full, use remaining points to remove opponents’ pieces by selecting them. Rolling 5 (the rare point landing, approximately 1% of rolls) wins immediately.

Click highlighted circles to spend each point. You can choose where to remove opposing pieces. Players who lose all their pieces are eliminated. In team mode, you control two territories against two AI-controlled territories; teammates cannot capture each other, and the last team with pieces wins.

Source: Adapted from the Ngulumapu version described in Kechukawe (Scribd). The interactive turn handling and automatic opponents are modern design choices, not claims about historical practice.
